## What changed and why

The article follows one argument: website tasks establish interaction, desktop tasks expose whether files and entire workflows finish, and browser-only jobs raise a separate infrastructure choice. The four reviews remain ordered Claude, OpenAI, Sai, UI-TARS. TinyFish's own browser section leads into authenticated work, engineering ownership and session durability.

**Browser Use is excluded because the requested editorial scope changed**, not because its results were invalid. Its comparison entries, recommendations and screenshots are removed; its private evidence is untouched. Genuine benchmark target URLs still contain the repository's name. Selecting four systems doesn't imply a newly rerun four-agent study.

TinyFish is separate because it is a browser agent, not a participant in the native desktop batch. It was part of the original exploratory browser study. Claude's strong browser results and TinyFish's managed execution justified the closer comparison; they weren't declared the two best agents overall.

## Strongest findings

- OpenAI completed five desktop checklists. Claude completed three including a labeled recovery retry; its correct CSV and unfinished reopening show why a saved file isn't the entire workflow.
- Both completed three clean supplier workflows; Claude was faster and cheaper in recorded API usage.
- TinyFish removed our browser host and low-level feedback loop. Source footprint demonstrates an ownership difference, not measured labor savings.
- TinyFish's saved Profile and Claude's persistent Firefox volume both restored authentication. The disposable container didn't; managed persistence wasn't unique persistence.
